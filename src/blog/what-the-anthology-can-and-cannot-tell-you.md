The Anthology is the record of every paper presented at an EISS conference since 2017. It now holds 511 of them across twelve editions, tagged to seventeen research themes, and it is the first thing we point a new volunteer at. It removes the blank page. Someone writing on deterrence starts from 46 papers, their authors and, where we have them, their abstracts.

It is also the sort of object that invites a bad question. A tagged corpus with counts against it looks like a map of a field, and the counts are easy to lift into a sentence beginning "European security studies has been increasingly concerned with". Before anyone writes that sentence, including us, it is worth being precise about what the numbers are.

## The coverage cliff is the first thing to know

Abstract coverage is not even across the corpus. From 2023 onwards, 219 of 236 papers carry an abstract. Before 2023, 75 of 275 do.

That is 93 per cent against 27 per cent, and it is not a statement about the papers. It is a statement about how we collected them. From 2023 the conference has run on Indico, where authors submit an abstract as part of the process, so the abstract exists because the submission form required it. Before that, the record was reconstructed from conference programmes, which carry titles and names and very little else. Recovering the missing ones means contacting authors or reading the organisers' files, one paper at a time, which is precisely why it is a volunteer task and not a script.

So any analysis that leans on abstract text is, silently, an analysis of the last four years. It will look like a finding about a decade.

## The themes describe panels, not papers

The seventeen themes are assigned at panel level. A paper inherits the theme of the panel it was scheduled in, because that is the level at which the programme actually commits to a subject.

This is a reasonable way to tag a conference and a poor way to classify a paper. Panels are assembled under real constraints: a convener has rooms to fill, slots to balance and a paper that fits two panels goes in the one with space. A paper on the political economy of drone procurement sits under whichever heading its panel took, and the tag records that decision rather than the paper's argument.

Thirty-two papers carry no theme at all, because their panel title matched no rule. We leave them untagged rather than guessing, which is the honest option and also an unhelpful one if you are counting.

## What the counts are good for

Read as a description of the EISS programme, the distribution is informative. Transformations of warfare and conflict is the largest theme at 76 papers, followed by emerging domains at 72 and defence cooperation at 68. At the other end, gender and security carries 6 papers, climate and security 10, and the political economy of security 12.

Those small numbers are the ones most likely to be misread. Six papers on gender and security is a fact about what has been submitted to and programmed at this conference over nine years. It is not a measurement of the field, and treating it as one would be both wrong and unkind to the people working in it. The interesting question it raises is about our own call for papers, and that is a question for us rather than a finding about anyone else.

Used carefully, the corpus answers questions of the form "what has this conference been about", "who has presented here more than once" and "which papers sit next to each other often enough to suggest a conversation". Those are real questions and nobody else can answer them, because nobody else holds the record.

## Why we are saying this first

This is the first post on a new blog, and most of what follows will be written by volunteers and interns working with exactly this material. It seemed better to start by marking the edges than to let someone discover them in a draft.

The standard we will hold each other to is the one this post is trying to demonstrate. State the coverage. Say which years the evidence actually covers. When a count is small, say why that might be, and resist the temptation to make it mean something.

The corpus is described in full, with its sources and its stated limitations, in the [corpus note](https://hal.science/hal-05711925), and the data behind it is [deposited and citable](/licensing.html). If you find a paper we have wrong, or an abstract we are missing, [tell us](/internship.html).
